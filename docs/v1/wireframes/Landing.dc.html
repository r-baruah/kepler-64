AN OPEN-SOURCE CHESS ENGINE · GEN 4

A tiny universe that learned to play chess.

Kepler-64 knows no chess advice. Its only judgement is one physical field: every piece pulls along the lines it can move, and the pull stops at the first piece in the way. A dozen numbers set how strong each law is, and it learned them by playing itself.

01 · THE ONE LAW

Pull follows the moves.

Space in Kepler's universe is shaped by the rules of chess. A rook pulls along its file and rank, a knight in its L-shapes, and the pull stops at the first piece in the way. Here, the rook's pull reaches its own pawn and goes no further.

Playing with this field beats counting material alone by 330 Elo (engine against engine, 4,000 games).

02 · LEARNED, NOT TOLD

It found the piece values by itself.

We started a universe where every piece weighed the same. After 60,000 games against itself it had sorted them: queen, rook, bishop, knight. A second universe, with different random games, landed on the same values.

03 · THE LAW LEDGER

Every law has to earn its place.

04 · HOW STRONG

Measured against a known yardstick.

Gen 3 played 200 games at each strength setting of Stockfish 19, the standard open-source engine, deliberately limited. It holds level with the 2100 setting.

Stockfish's own strength scale, 30 seconds plus 0.3 per move. Not a human rating. The opponent in your browser searches less per move; its own numbers are measured before launch.

05 · REBUILT HONESTLY

The first version had a bigger story than its physics.

Version 0 talked about tidal forces and black-hole queens. When I measured it on 120 test positions, removing that physics changed none of its moves. So I rebuilt the universe from scratch, in Rust, with one rule: a law stays only if it wins games. The laws that didn't are in the table above.
